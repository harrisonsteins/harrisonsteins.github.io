About

Physician. Researcher.
Educator Without Borders.

I am an incoming Emergency Medicine Resident Physician with advanced training at the intersection of critical care, wilderness medicine, and global health. My academic credentials span a Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine (DO) from Rocky Vista University, a Master of Science in Biomedical Science (MS), a Master of Science in Austere Critical Care (MSc) from the College of Remote and Offshore Medicine in Malta — where my thesis investigated point-of-care ultrasound diagnosis of Swimming-Induced Pulmonary Edema in endurance athletes at altitude — and a Fellowship in the Academy of Wilderness Medicine (FAWM).

I am the inaugural Predoctoral Clinical Ultrasound Scholar at Rocky Vista University, selected by faculty to develop and teach ultrasound curriculum and pursue clinical research. I currently serve as a Faculty Lecturer at the College of Remote and Offshore Medicine for the MSc in Austere Critical Care program, and as a Wilderness Medicine Instructor with the Colorado Mountain Club.

Prior to medical school, I served as Medical Logistics Chief for San Mateo County's COVID-19 Emergency Operations Center — coordinating clinic sites vaccinating 4,000 people/day, totaling 870,000 individuals and 2 million vaccines — and founded the San Mateo County Medical Reserve Corps. As Medical Operations Manager for CrowdRx, I designed and operated a 53-foot mobile ICU trailer with 14 ICU beds and 2 resuscitation bays for mass gathering events including Burning Man.

My research has taken me from IronMan races at altitude to an emergency department in Tanzania to mountain medicine conferences across North America and Europe. I bring the same rigor to every setting: evidence, improvisation, and resourcefulness.

Austere & Resource-Limited Medicine
Medicine doesn't stop working when the hospital is three days away. It just demands more of its practitioners.

Austere medicine is the practice of delivering high-quality critical care in environments where conventional tools, teams, and infrastructure are absent or severely limited — wilderness, high-altitude expeditions, disaster zones, conflict environments, and under-resourced emergency departments worldwide. This is my primary clinical and research identity.
